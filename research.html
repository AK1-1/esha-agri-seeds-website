Innovation at Core

Research & Development

Plant breeding is the foundation of Esha Agri Seeds — creating unique, marketable products through strong research programs.

Our Approach

Science-Driven Seed Innovation

A seed's success in the market depends primarily on its improved traits, which exemplify the R&D effort. Under seasoned breeders and researchers, we develop seeds embodying improvements such as high yields, resistance to disease and pests, and traits specific to regional agro-climatic conditions.

Esha Agri Seeds has breeding programs for vegetable crops in various segments, stationed at different agro-climatic zones. Main research activities are conducted at our head office in Hyderabad, Telangana, and at our vegetable research hub in Bangalore, India.

Hybrid varieties play a vital role in increasing vegetable production due to their high yield potential, early maturing, superior quality and pest-resistance attributes.

Breeding Programs

Crop Research Focus Areas

Detailed breeding programs targeting specific disease resistance and yield improvement across our key crops.

🍅 Tomato Research

Rapidly producing superior varieties combining resistance to 6+ disease targets — TYLCV, Fusarium Crown Rot, bacterial spot. Backcrossing resistance into elite breeding lines with proven hybrid performance, heat tolerance and superior flavour.

TYLCVFCR6+ Disease TargetsHeat Tolerance

🌶️ Chilli Research

Germplasm sources resistant to ChiLCV, ChiMoV, CMV, anthracnose, bacterial wilt, nematodes and powdery mildew. Nuclear and cytoplasmic male sterility systems for commercial hybrid seed production. Segments: fresh market, processing, dried spice, industrial extracts.

ChiLCVCMVMale Sterility4 Market Segments

🍉 Watermelon Research

Breeding for fusarium wilt, anthracnose, gummy stem blight, powdery mildew resistance. Traits: seedlessness, high lycopene, new flesh colours. Multiple segments including Jubilee, Allsweet, Crimson Sweet types.

Fusarium WiltSeedlessHigh LycopeneMulti-Segment

🟢 Okra Research

Improving yield under adverse conditions through resistant hybrids. YVMV disease resistance with drought tolerance screening. Novel drought tolerant lines through reverse genetics for sustainable okra farming.
